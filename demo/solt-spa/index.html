Salt air, then the float

Most people book both rooms, one after the other. This is the whole visit, from the glowing room to the pool.

Minute 0, the salt room

Walls of salt, lit from behind

The walls are built from blocks of rock salt with lamps behind them, so the room glows the colour of a low fire. The floor is loose salt, deep enough to push your bare feet into. Six loungers, blankets, no screens.

Minute 5

Air you can taste

A halogenerator on the wall mills pure salt into a dust finer than flour and lets it drift through the room. You won’t see it. After twenty minutes you taste it on your lips. Nobody talks much.

Minute 55, the float room

Then you float

After a shower, the pool: thirty centimetres of water with half a tonne of Epsom salt dissolved in it, kept at skin temperature. Lie back and it holds you up. The lights are yours to dim, all the way to dark.

Three ways in

We don’t sell salt as a cure; the research on salt air is thin. What we sell is an hour in which nothing asks anything of you.

Your first visit

Come ten minutes early and bring nothing. Robes, towels, slippers and earplugs are ours.

  1. 1Tea room. A cup of tea and a short form about your health.
  2. 2Lockers. Change into a robe. Phones stay here.
  3. 3Salt room. Forty-five minutes in a lounger.
  4. 4Float room. A shower, then sixty minutes alone, the door locked from inside.
  5. 5Tea room again. Stay as long as you like.

Before you come
Skip shaving and waxing on the day; salt finds every nick. If you are pregnant, have epilepsy or a heart condition, ask your doctor before you float.
