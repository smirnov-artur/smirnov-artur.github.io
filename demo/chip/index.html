02

What is actually being solved, in order.

Four steps. Each one feeds the next, and each one is on screen with your numbers substituted — no static illustration anywhere on this page.

Step 1 · geometry

Chip thickness and curl

a1 = s·sin κ
φ = 45° − (β − γ)/2
ξ = cos(φ − γ)/sin φ
a2 = a1·ξ

Merchant's shear-angle solution with γ = 8°, κ = 95°.

Step 2 · heat

Temperature in the zone

θ = 110·v⁰·⁴·s⁰·²·t⁰·¹⁰⁵

Empirical power law for carbide on medium-carbon steel.

Step 3 · colour

Why the chip turns blue

d = 1408·e^(−3393/T)
δ = 2·n·d·cos θr
I(λ) = |r1 + r2·e^(iδ)|²

Oxide film thickness from temperature, then two-beam interference evaluated separately at 630 / 532 / 465 nm — in the shader, per pixel, against the real view angle.

Step 4 · load

Force, power, tool life

Fc = kc1.1·a1^(1−mc)·b
Pc = Fc·v/60000
T = (C/(v·s^0.35·t^0.15))^4

Kienzle with kc1.1 = 1700 N/mm², mc = 0.25; Taylor with C = 270, n = 0.25.

The break rule is the one honest fudge here: a chip snaps when the bending strain ε = a2/4R passes the fracture strain εf, and εf rises with temperature because hot steel is ductile. Break length is fitted, not derived — L = 2πR/CB⁶, capped at 4…380 mm.
