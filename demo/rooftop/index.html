Painted planes, real light

Built as an answer to a lo-fi brief: flat planes mapped from a fixed projector camera, blended with actual geometry, and lit by lights that move. Nothing here is a texture or a model — every pixel is generated in GLSL, so the whole scene weighs less than one photograph.

The camera you look through is not the camera the picture was painted from. Each layer samples its content in the projector's clip space, so the composition stays pinned to the frame while the layers shear against each other as the camera drifts. Turn the toggle off and the painting goes back to being stuck on flat cards; open the layers and you can walk around the stack and watch the projection happen.

  • Five painted layers, projected from one fixed camera
  • Rooftop, viaduct and sign are real meshes on real normals
  • Three lights — sun, neon sign, passing train — reach both halves
  • Hand-written GLSL throughout: skyline, windows, grade, grain
  • Bloom, split-tone grade and 12 fps film grain in one pass

One evening, from scratch · Three.js + own GLSL · no textures, no models
