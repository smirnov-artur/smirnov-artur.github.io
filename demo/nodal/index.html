01 — how the figure is computed

Nothing here is drawn by hand. The lines are the zeros of one function.

A thin square plate with free edges has, to a good first approximation, standing waves of the form below. The grains bounce wherever the plate moves and stay wherever it does not, so after a second and a half of bowing they have traced the set ψ = 0 for you.

// displacement of the plate, x and y in [0,1]
ψ(x, y) = cos(nπx)·cos(mπy) − cos(mπx)·cos(nπy)

// nodal lines — where the sand settles
ψ(x, y) = 0

// Kirchhoff plate: frequency grows with the sum of squares
f(m, n) = f₀ · (m² + n²),   f₀ = 36 Hz

// what the GPU integrates for each of 262 144 grains
v.xz ← mix(v.xz, −∇|ψ| · drift · R, 1 − e^(−6Δt))
v.y  ← |ψ| · A   on contact,   then  v ← v · e^(−2.6Δt)
512 × 512 GPGPU texture ∇|ψ| by central differences, h = 0.0035 R = resonance weight, e^(−((f−f₍ₘₙ₎)/w)²) A = bow amplitude

The audible tone is built from the same numbers: the fundamental is f(m,n), and the three partials above it are f(m+1,n), f(m,n+1) and f(m+1,n+1) — which is why the timbre thins out as the sweep climbs. Sound stays off until you switch it on.

02 — three states of the same plate

Off resonance, on resonance, and one second after a strike.

01742 Hz

Between modes

No mode is close, so the plate has no still places. The grains take a slow random walk and the layer stays even.

02900 Hz

On mode (4,3)

Resonance weight reaches 1. Within about one and a half seconds every grain has left the antinodes and the curve ψ = 0 is visible.

03900 Hz + strike

One second after a strike

A press throws the whole layer up at once. The figure survives — it re-forms from the outside in, because the outer antinodes throw hardest.
