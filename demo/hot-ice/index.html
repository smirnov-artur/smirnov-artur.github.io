01

Three rules, and none of them is noise

The branching you see is not a fractal texture pasted over a circle. It falls out of a phase field solved live on the GPU at 384² — one cell is 0.23 mm of real dish — where the only inputs are a neighbour test, a growth law and a heat balance.

The front

A cell can only freeze if a neighbouring cell is already solid and its own temperature is below the melting point. The deeper the undercooling, the faster it goes — this is the whole reason the slider changes the speed.

v = k · (Tm − T)^1.5 · a(θ)

now: 0.00 mm/s at 36 K undercooling

Latent heat

Freezing releases energy. Each cell that solidifies dumps L/c = 94 K worth of heat into its own texel, which pushes the local temperature back up towards Tm and stops the front there. The tip that happens to stick out finds cold liquid, runs faster, and the arm is born. That instability is the dendrite — it is not painted on.

ΔT_local = (L / c) · Δφ = 94.3 K · Δφ

instant adiabatic share: 38.2 % of the dish

Grains

Every seed carries a lattice orientation drawn at random. Growth is 6-fold anisotropic around that orientation, and a freezing cell inherits it from whichever neighbour reached it first. Two seeds therefore grow two lattices, and where they meet the mismatch stays in the surface forever — the grain boundary.

a(θ) = 1 + 0.45 · cos 6(ψ − θ_grain)

02

Recalescence: the thing gets hotter while it freezes

This is the claim worth checking. The blue line is the solid fraction, the orange line is the mean temperature of the dish. Both are read back off the GPU field — the 384² texture is reduced to 32² on the card and pulled to the CPU eight times a second, so the plot is a measurement of the simulation, not a replay of a script.

mean temperature, °C solid fraction, % waiting for the first seed

One honest caveat, because it changes what you are looking at. In sodium acetate the thermal length D/v is 0.018 mm — fifty times finer than a cell of this field, so a literal simulation would draw a smooth wall and no arms at all. The field is therefore solved in the standard dimensionless phase-field units, scaled so the thermal length lands on three cells. Everything physical stays physical — °C, the 264 J/g of latent heat, the energy balance that caps how much can freeze at once — and the price is time: the dish runs at 0.75× of real speed, and every mm/s on the gauges is divided back out.

The temperature cannot pass Tm: at the melting point the driving force is zero and growth stops. So the curve climbs steeply, flattens on a plateau, and the rest of the dish can only freeze as fast as the glass and the air carry the heat away. That plateau is the entire product below.

Undercooling is a property of the charge, not a setting: sodium acetate holds 30–40 K of it for months in a sealed pouch, which is exactly why it is the one that ships. Water in a clean vessel manages 10–15 K and loses it to the first speck of dust. The dish above will still let you run water — the front simply goes somewhere else.
